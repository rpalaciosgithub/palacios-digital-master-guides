Power Verb Recognition Warm-Up
LEARN THE 10 POWER VERBS FIRST
What are Power Verbs? Open / Close
Consider this sentence:

Yo quiero comer pizza.
I want to eat pizza.

Let's break down every piece of it.

quiero — this is the Power Verb.

It changes its ending to match who's talking. Since yo (I) is talking, the verb becomes quiero.

If it were tú (you), it would change to quieres.

The Power Verb always adjusts to match the subject.

comer — this word means “to eat.” It is called an infinitive.

An infinitive is a verb in its raw, original, dictionary form — the form you'd find if you looked it up in a Spanish dictionary. Infinitives never change, no matter who's doing the action.

In Spanish, you recognize an infinitive because it ends in -ar, -er, or -ir.

Comer ends in -er, so it's an infinitive.

In English, infinitives are usually recognized by the word “to” in front of the verb — “to eat,” “to speak,” “to swim,” “to want.”

That little word “to” is the English signal for an infinitive, the same way -ar / -er / -ir is the Spanish signal.

So the full sentence breaks down like this:

quiero = Power Verb (changes to match “yo”)
comer = infinitive, ends in -er, means “to eat,” never changes

Now a second example, same method:

Me gusta nadar.
I like to swim.

gusta — the Power Verb. It matches “yo” through me gusta (literally “it is pleasing to me”).

nadar — this is the infinitive. It ends in -ar, and it means “to swim.”

Notice the English translation has “to” in front of “swim” — that's your signal it's an infinitive in English too.

Putting it all together — the definition:

A Power Verb is the verb in a sentence that changes to match who's talking.

Right after it comes a second verb called an infinitive — the plain, unchanging, dictionary form of a verb.

In Spanish, you spot an infinitive because it ends in -ar, -er, or -ir (like comer or nadar).

In English, you spot an infinitive because it has “to” in front of it (like “to eat” or “to swim”).

The Power Verb does all the changing; the infinitive never changes, no matter who's doing the action.

KEEP YO / TÚ VISIBLE
At first, use the subject because it makes the form easier to understand:
I want
→
YO QUIERO
You want (informal)
→
TÚ QUIERES
Spanish can omit YO or TÚ when the person is already clear.

Yo soy Esteban. Soy de Estados Unidos.
I am Esteban. I am from the United States.

Tú eres Mario. Eres mi mejor amigo.
You are Mario. You are my best friend.

The first sentence makes the person clear. The next sentence does not need to repeat the subject.
